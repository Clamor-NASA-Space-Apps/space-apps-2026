# Moving on Mars, and what Gale crater looks like 🔴

For the game's reality sheet (every number a player can feel) and for art direction. Saved sources are under `pages/agent-4/` and `../junior-astronaut/pages/`.

> [!TIP]
> **TL;DR.** Nobody has measured a person walking on Mars. The honest numbers come from Apollo and from NASA suit tests: about 1 m/s walking, a 0.4 m jump on the Moon in a full suit, and rovers that are far slower than games make them. Gale is a butterscotch sky over grey rock dusted red, with a 5 km mountain in the middle.

---

## 1. Walking in a suit 🚶

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Mars surface gravity | 3.73 m/s² mean, 0.38 g | NASA Mars Fact Sheet |
| Apollo walking, a plausible average | 3.6 km/h, which is 1 m/s | Apollo Lunar Surface Journal |
| Apollo sustained running | above 5 km/h (1.4 m/s) | same |
| Apollo 15, working around stops | 1.1 to 2.2 km/h | same |
| Stopping | "two or three steps to come comfortably to a stable stationary position" (Aldrin) | same |
| Suited walk-to-run change, Moon | Froude number 0.36 ± 0.11, from 38 Apollo gait events | Carr and McGee 2009 |
| NASA suit test in Mars gravity | the Mark III suit "was extremely limited on Mars", and moving cost more energy than in Moon gravity | NASA walkback test, 2008 |
| Suit mass | Apollo 89 kg; newest design 177 kg; a Mars suit should not exceed 104 kg | NASA suit paper, July 2026 |
| Walking range on one charge | "roughly 20 km total" | NASA Mars design study (DRA 5.0) |

The Froude number is speed² divided by (gravity × leg length). It says when legs stop walking and start running. For a 0.9 m leg on Mars, 0.36 gives 1.1 m/s and the unsuited value of 0.5 gives 1.3 m/s. That is our own arithmetic from Moon data, not a measurement.

A computer model (Ackermann and van den Bogert 2012) predicted walking at 1.1 m/s in Mars gravity, and running or skipping at 2.0 m/s. Bunny hopping is a Moon habit.

**For the sheet:** walk near 1.0 m/s, hurry near 1.4 to 2.0 m/s, slow to start and two or three steps to stop. Mark the Mars values "estimate" with the arithmetic shown.

**Kid version:** in a heavy suit you walk about as fast as a slow stroll, and you cannot stop on the spot.

## 2. Jumping 🦘

- Apollo 16, John Young's salute jump: launched at about 1.17 m/s, "reached a maximum height of 0.42 m", in the air about 1.45 seconds.
- The same push on Mars gives 0.18 m and 0.63 seconds. Our arithmetic: 1.17² ÷ (2 × 3.73).
- Charlie Duke jumped as high as he could, "tipped over backwards" and landed on his life-support backpack.
- So a suited Mars jump is a small hop. A 0.5 m jump needs a 1.9 m/s launch, well above the one measured suited jump.

## 3. Rovers 🚙

| Vehicle | Speed | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Perseverance, top speed | 152 m per hour | NASA |
| Curiosity, driving itself | 15 to 18 m per hour | JPL |
| Perseverance, driving itself | about 110 m per hour | JPL |
| NASA crewed rover concept (Space Exploration Vehicle) | about 10 km/h, crew of two | NASA fact sheet |
| Apollo 17 lunar rover | 35.9 km in 4 h 26 min, about 8 km/h | NASA |

A crewed rover in the game can honestly do 10 km/h (2.8 m/s). A robot rover is a crawl: speed it up for play, and say so on screen.

## 4. Dust underfoot 🌫️

The unverified document says kicked dust falls back in neat arcs. The sources say otherwise for fine dust: clouds raised by the Ingenuity helicopter "became diffuse within seconds" and drifted downwind at 4 to 6 m/s. Storm dust takes "weeks or months to settle". Draw a small puff that drifts and thins, not an arc.

## 5. What Gale crater looks like 🏔️

| Feature | Fact | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Day sky | "an orange or reddish color" | NASA Space Place |
| Sunset | blue-grey around the Sun, because fine dust lets blue light through near the Sun's direction | NASA |
| Sunlight | 43% of Earth's (586 W/m²) | factcheck row 15 |
| Rock under the dust | grey: a drilled sample was "gray-green powder" beside red windblown dust | NASA, 2013 |
| Rock types | layered sandstone, mudstone from ancient lakes, pebbly conglomerate, dark basalt sand dunes | Curiosity science papers |
| Crater width | 154 km | NASA |
| Mount Sharp | about 5 km high, in the middle | NASA |
| Crater rim on the horizon | 30 to 40 km away, depending on where the rover stood | NASA |
| Flat-ground horizon | about 3.3 km at eye height. Our arithmetic from the planet's radius | |
| Normal haze | dust opacity 0.3 to 0.7 | Vasavada 2022 |
| 2018 dust storm at Gale | opacity 8.5, the sky six to eight times thicker with haze, light turned "cherry red", ultraviolet down 97% | NASA |
| Dust devils | seen crossing the crater floor on summer afternoons | NASA, 2017 |

**For the look:** a butterscotch sky that pales toward the horizon, grey-brown rock with a red dust coat, a mountain and a far rim rising above a close horizon, and a storm that dims and reddens everything without making it black.
